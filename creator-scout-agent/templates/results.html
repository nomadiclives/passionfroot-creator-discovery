What this page is for

This is the answer to the brief: every creator in the pool, scored out of 100 against the five things the campaign cares about, ranked, and given a role in the campaign. The people worth approaching are at the top of the big table, numbered.

  1. Read the five counters below. They split the pool into shortlisted, and four kinds of not-yet — each explained under the counters.
  2. Work down the table. Every row carries its own score breakdown and, where it was not shortlisted, the reason in plain words.
  3. Export the CSV to take the shortlist elsewhere, or go to the judging screen to supply whatever human judgement is still missing.

Everything evaluated stays on the page, including creators the engine refused to score. A row that quietly disappeared would look the same as a creator nobody ever went looking for, and those are very different problems.

What each status means

Every row in the table further down carries one of these five. Three of them are not verdicts on the creator at all — they are the app telling you what it is still missing.

Keep
Scored, cleared every gate, and ranked into the shortlist. Ready to approach.
Drop
Ruled out, and the row says why: they cannot make the deliverable this campaign needs, they are under an active exclusivity clause someone has recorded, they are outside the follower band or not on a briefed platform, their engagement looks inauthentic, or they scored below 35 out of 100. The reason printed on the row is the authority here. Usually final. Note that being sponsored by a competitor is not on that list. It raises a flag on the row instead, because a creator a rival has already paid takes sponsorships and can run a brief — that is a lead worth a look, not a rejection.
NEEDS_REFRESH
A required number is missing — a follower count, or the rate that says whether the audience actually shows up. Without it there is nothing honest to score, so the creator is held here until someone sources the real figure. The app will never estimate one to fill the gap.
NEEDS_REVIEW
The numbers are there, but a human call is not — the four judged scores, or the read on how familiar this creator's audience already is with {{ criteria.category }}. A judgement recorded about a different product category also lands here rather than being carried across. Fill it in on the judging screen.
NEEDS_CALIBRATION
Nothing is wrong with the creator. Their platform has no scoring bands fitted yet (LinkedIn, today), so the app cannot say whether a given rate is good or bad without inventing the boundary — and it would rather say so.
